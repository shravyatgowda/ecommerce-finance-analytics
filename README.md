# E-commerce Finance Analytics

Three finance analysis workflows for an e-commerce business, built with **Python (pandas)** and **SQL**:

1. **COD reconciliation**: match carrier Cash on Delivery reports against internal order books and quantify payment leakage
2. **Accounts receivable aging**: bucket outstanding invoices into 30/60/90-day intervals
3. **Month-end variance analysis**: compare actual operating expenses with budget and flag overruns

> **Data disclaimer:** all data in this repository is **synthetic**, generated by `src/generate_synthetic_data.py`. It mirrors the scale and structure of the real reconciliation work I did during my Data Analytics training and freelance projects (35,000+ COD transactions, a receivables book of roughly 150K, month-end expense variance), but contains **no client or employer data**. The numbers below come from the synthetic data and demonstrate the method.

## Why this matters

For cash-on-delivery businesses, money is lost quietly: an order is marked return-to-origin (RTO) internally while the carrier has actually collected cash, or a carrier remits less than it collected. Receivables go overdue without anyone seeing it, and costs drift from budget. These scripts make each of those visible.

## Method

**COD reconciliation:** outer-join orders to the carrier report on `order_id`, then classify each order. *RTO status mismatch* means the internal status is RTO but the carrier shows delivered. *Short remittance* means a delivered order where remitted cash is below collected cash. Leakage is the unremitted amount, and the rate is leakage divided by total COD collected by carriers.

**AR aging:** outstanding = invoice amount minus amount paid. Days overdue = as-of date minus due date. Buckets: Current, 1-30, 31-60, 61-90 and 90+ days. Summaries are produced overall and per customer.

**Variance:** variance = actual minus budget, expressed as a percentage of budget. Category-months above +5% are flagged `OVERRUN`; below -5% are `UNDERSPEND`.

## Limitations and next steps

- Synthetic data is cleaner than real carrier files, which need extra cleaning (formatting, duplicate AWB numbers, date mismatches).
- The AR model assumes no partial payments in the sample; the code already handles them through `amount_paid`.
- Possible extensions: a Metabase dashboard on top of the output tables, automated monthly runs, and a carrier scorecard.
